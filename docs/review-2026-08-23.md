# Adversarial review — 2026-08-23

A full-system review against the owner's actual goals: **(1) trustworthy historical energy
usage, (2) spotting anomalies, (3) verifying the utility bill to within ~5%.** This is a
home system; findings are scoped to that — nothing here asks for SaaS hardening.

**How it was done:** five parallel review passes (numeric core; sources; query/billing
surface; runtime/ops; fitness-for-purpose), plus live probing of the real S3 archive with
DuckDB and the Lightsail `/healthz` endpoint. No code was changed. All line numbers are as
of `main` @ `787cbb9` plus the uncommitted #179/#180/#181 work.

---

## Verdict in one paragraph

The data core is genuinely trustworthy — measurably so, not just by inspection (see
"Verified live" below). Goal 1 is well served. The two big gaps are **not in the data, they
are around it**: (a) every failure signal this system carefully produces terminates in
`/healthz` and `status.json`, which nothing reads — the system has detection everywhere and
delivery nowhere; and (b) anomaly detection and dollar-level bill verification are not
built at all — the archive can support both, but today the owner is the anomaly detector
and the bill check stops at kWh. Below that sit a handful of real bugs and gate-holes that
can make the billing comparison silently wrong, and a long tail of documentation drift.

## The findings

Grouped by what they threaten. Severity is relative to the three goals.

### A. Nothing is watching (the single biggest gap)

**A1 — HIGH. There is no alerting path at all.** The only automated consumer of
`/healthz` is the Docker healthcheck (`docker-compose.yml:69-74`), and Docker only flips a
label — it never restarts or notifies. No cron, no email, no uptime ping exists anywhere
in the repo or deploy docs. Worse, `/healthz` returns **200** for most of the failure
modes that matter: S3 creds rotated (upload fails hourly, spool grows, healthz green),
LG&E revoked post-#177 (raises loudly into `job_failed` — which nothing reads), LG&E
publishing nothing (`health.meter.stale: true` in a body nobody fetches), a hung batch job
(no per-job timeout, `runtime.py:310-317`). The two recent real incidents — the 3-day LG&E
lapse and the 6-day #180 CT latch — both ran under a green healthcheck and were found by a
human looking at a chart. Every hardening since #177 improved *visibility*, none added
*delivery*.
**Fix (boring, one evening):** a cron on the Mac (it is inside the allowed house IP):
`curl -sf http://13.219.164.226:8080/healthz | jq -e '<checks>' || <notify>` against
`health.ok`, `health.meter.stale`, max `consecutive_failures`, and uploader age — or a
free healthchecks.io/ntfy ping. One line of infrastructure closes the whole class.

**A2 — HIGH (live, confirmed on the instance right now). The collector schedules rollup
and compaction it is forbidden to run.** `default_jobs()` still returns all five jobs, but
the collector IAM user is (correctly, by design) denied `energy/hourly/`. Result observed
live: `rollup.consecutive_failures: 6` and climbing, one `AccessDenied` per hour forever;
`compactor.last_success_utc: null`. Consequences: **`energy_hourly` is stale** (last hour
2026-08-22 23:00 local — today's rollups exist only when someone manually runs them from
the Mac); today's parts (16 so far) will not be compacted tonight; and the permanent
failure noise will bury a *real* new failure in the same counters. This is the
already-agreed "split the poller from the batch stages" work (STATE.md) — but until the
split lands, either give the nightly/hourly derived-data jobs a home that has credentials,
or add the job-selection knob now so the instance stops attempting what it cannot do.

**A3 — MEDIUM. Uploader failure is invisible to everything that could react.**
`/healthz` deliberately judges only the two pollers (`health.py:106-109`);
`Scheduler._record_failure` writes to a shared `scheduler` section nothing resets or
judges. Rotated S3 keys → green healthz indefinitely while the archive quietly stops
growing. Fix: a reported-never-503 block (like `health.meter`) for uploader age and
`spool.pending_rows`, so A1's cron can see it.

**A4 — MEDIUM. `/healthz` liveness measures cycle success, not rows landed.**
`poller.py:404` stamps success on a zero-row cycle. A collector "succeeding" while
emitting nothing is 200-healthy forever. Fix: report `last_row_utc` per poller.

**A5 — MEDIUM. An outage longer than 3 days strands compaction/rollup for the older
days.** `daily_maintenance` only looks back `today-3..today-1` (`runtime.py:150`). After a
5-day failure, D-5/D-4 get uploaded but never compacted or rolled — holes in
`energy_hourly` that read exactly like collector downtime. Recovery exists but requires
remembering. Fix: derive the range from evidence (days with parts but no day file).

### B. The bill check (goal 3) — machinery good, gates leaky, dollars absent

The end-to-end machinery is consistent: `compare-meter` reuses the production rollup SQL,
pins the 900s series, refuses to guess between meters, separates house from barn. Over
clean hours the panels-vs-meter agreement is inside the ±5% goal. But:

**B0 — Fact correction: the CTs read LOW, not high.** STATE.md:140 says "the feed CTs
read ~3.4% **high** against the meter"; every measurement including STATE's own
(77.614 meter vs 75.186 panels = −3.1%) and all six days probed live shows panels *below*
the meter. Direction matters for interpreting a bill dispute: a bill that looks ~3-5%
higher than the panels is expected CT under-read, not over-billing. Fix the sentence.

Live day-by-day (900s house meter vs summed feed CTs):

| local day | meter kWh | panels kWh | Δ% |
|---|---|---|---|
| 08-18 | 77.61 | 75.19 | −3.1 |
| 08-19 | 77.21 | 73.49 | −4.8 |
| 08-20 | 75.98 | 72.37 | −4.7 |
| 08-21 | 71.85 | 69.66 | −3.1 |
| 08-22 | 72.27 | 66.99 | **−7.3** |

**B1 — HIGH. `compare-meter`'s coverage gate is blind to a wholly-missing hub.**
`compare.py:123-162` takes the min `sample_count` across channels *that produced rows* —
a hub absent all hour contributes nothing to the min. One hub offline for a day →
"panels read ~50% below the meter" at 100% coverage, published by the CLI and the `/ui`
card. `historyview.meter_block` already has the honest fix (`series_seen ==
expected_series` from dim_channel, historyview.py:507-556); `compare.py`/`meterview.py`
do not. Port it.

**B2 — HIGH. `/ui/history`'s meter delta gates only the panel side.** A partially
published meter day (LG&E publishes late and revises) yields e.g. +65% `delta_pct` marked
`complete: true`, and it pollutes `mean_delta_pct` — the headline KPI. `intervals` is
carried per row but never checked. Fix: require the DST-aware full interval count
(92/96/100 for 900s) before publishing a delta.

**B3 — HIGH (known, #180, unguarded). The Panel B CT zero-latch defeats every coverage
gate**, because latched zeros are real samples — full `sample_count`, hours reading −13%
to −22%, indistinguishable on every surface from "the meter over-records". DEVIATIONS
#180 states the query-time rule; no view implements it, so `mean_delta_pct` and the meter
card currently mix instrument-fault days with clean ones. The A/B experiment is the right
next step; when the fix lands (latched zeros → honest gaps), the existing coverage gates
start working on their own. Until then, treat the automated comparison surfaces as
polluted and trust `compare-meter` over clean ranges only. Related: **archive rows do not
record their transport** (`value_source` never reaches parquet), so the 08-17..23 Panel B
feed data stays permanently ambiguous between honest zero and latch.

**B4 — MEDIUM (live). One physically-contaminated hour drags a whole day outside ±5% and
nothing flags it.** 2026-08-22 10:00 local: meter 5.68 kWh, panels 2.91 kWh (−48.7%) at
*full* sample coverage — the breaker-retrofit work (CTs disturbed / panel work while the
meter kept counting). That one hour is most of why 08-22 shows −7.3%. There is no
day-level outlier surfacing anywhere — a per-hour |Δ| screen or a "worst hour" column on
the meter card would have named this instantly, and it is the same mechanism that would
catch a future CT drifting.

**B5 — HIGH (fitness). There is no billing-period concept and no dollars.** LG&E bills on
meter-read cycles; nothing stores cycle dates, sums the cycle, or accepts the bill's
printed kWh/dollars for a delta. The only USD anywhere is `cost_day_usd` — Carrier's own
HVAC-only estimate at whatever rate is typed into the thermostat; it looks like billing
data and must never be treated as such. Today the owner can verify a bill's *kWh*, not
its *amount*. Fix, in order: (1) a `verify-bill` command — inputs: cycle start/end + the
bill's kWh (and optionally $): ~90% of `compare.py` re-bucketed to a date range; (2) a
small hand-maintained tariff JSON ($/kWh by season, basic charge, riders) — at ±5%
tolerance an annually-updated static rate is entirely sufficient.

**B6 — MEDIUM. The documented billing recipe errors as written.** README's meter-vs-panels
steps fetch both meters then run `compare-meter` with no `--meter` → guaranteed
`AmbiguousMeterError`. `compare.run` never consults the map's `primary` flag (meterview
does). Fix: default to the map's primary, or add `--meter 1308468` to the README.

**B7 — MEDIUM. Nothing enforces at-most-one `primary` per source.** `dim.py:510` coerces
with `bool()` (`"primary": "no"` → True) and two primaries are accepted — which would make
`historyview` silently sum **house + barn**, the exact never-sum trap. Fix: build error on
non-bool `primary` and >1 primary.

### C. Historical-data correctness (goal 1) — strong, three durable risks

**C1 — HIGH (conditional, the one real time bomb in the math). `POLL_INTERVAL_S` is a
runtime knob, not a property of the data.** `rollup.py:177-183` reads the *current*
setting; nothing records the interval per row/file/hourly row, and nothing cross-checks
it against actual sample spacing. Set the interval to 60s next year, then follow the
documented repair path ("re-run rollup over the range") across 2026 days → **every
historical kWh doubles, deterministically, with zero warnings.** `test_rollup.py:258`
pins the mechanism rather than guarding it. Fix: store `observed_seconds` in
`HOURLY_SCHEMA`, or have `rollup_day` compare the configured interval to the median
`ts_utc` delta per hour and refuse loudly on mismatch. Cheap now, near-impossible to
untangle after a config change.

**C2 — MEDIUM. Green Button `--assume-uom` silently disables the reverse-flow filter.**
`stages/greenbutton.py:293-299`: the override carries `flow_direction=None`, which passes
the filter for every block, and LG&E pairs every UsagePoint with a reverse series —
first-in-document-order wins the merge, so real consumption can be replaced by the
reverse series under the same dedupe key. Fix: resolve the real ReadingType's
flow_direction even under an override. (The normal path's 4,198 skipped reverse rows were
verified correct — no generation at this house.)

**C3 — LOW-MEDIUM. Rollup vs compactor duplicate precedence disagree.** `rollup.sql`
breaks dedupe ties on smallest value; the compactor lets a corrected re-uploaded part
beat the day file. A manual `upload --force` repair followed by `rollup` *without*
`compact-daily` keeps the stale value until compaction. Fix: prefer `part-*` over `day-*`
in the SQL's `DISTINCT ON` ordering — or just document "force-repair order: upload →
compact → rollup".

**C4 — LOW. kWh undercounts when cadence degrades** (each sample charged exactly 30s;
slow cycles = fewer, sparser samples). Inherent to the locked observed-time formula,
observable via `sample_count`; recorded here so nobody rediscovers it as a bug.

### D. Silent-failure bugs found in code (fix-worthy regardless of theme)

**D1 — HIGH. An empty Green Button fetch erases the #177 freshness signal.**
`greenbutton_fetch.py:196-201` passes `newest_interval_utc=None` on a zero-row fetch;
`record_success` merges the null over the stored timestamp and the `health.meter` block
**vanishes from `/healthz`** — exactly in the state it was built to expose (LG&E stops
publishing without a revocation). One-line fix: only pass `newest_interval_utc` when
non-None. The watchdog currently deletes itself on duty.

**D2 — MEDIUM. A refresh-grant 400 destroys the LG&E refresh token without checking
why.** `lge_auth.py:578-591` clears the cache on any 400/401/403 — but `invalid_client`
(a rotated/mistyped `LGE_CLIENT_SECRET`) arrives as 400/401 too, burning a still-valid
customer authorization over a config error and forcing the manual browser re-auth #177
exists to avoid. Carrier's ladder already distinguishes `invalid_grant`; mirror it.

**D3 — MEDIUM. The proactive-refresh fix narrows but does not close the lapse window.**
Refresh only happens as a side-effect of the 09:15 job succeeding end-to-end; a missed
morning (host down, Okta down at 09:15, scheduler wedge) extends the refresh token's idle
time a day at a time, and if the real revocation rule is absolute lifetime the margin
change does nothing. Cheap hardening: retry a failed `greenbutton_daily` after a few
hours, decoupling refresh cadence from fetch success.

**D4 — LOW-MEDIUM. A legitimately empty ESPI window fails the nightly job with a
misleading "is it the CSV?" error** (`greenbutton.py:281-285`) — a fresh authorization
before first publication would fail every morning, loudly but wrongly. Treat a
well-formed feed with zero IntervalBlocks as zero rows on the fetch path.

**D5 — MEDIUM. `hvacview` still assumes the pre-#179 model.** It reads `stage_pct` only
and counts capacity-absent + breaker>25W as a "disagreement" — post-#179, `dehumidify`
arrives as a *word* (1,254 rows/6 days) while the compressor draws real power, so a
healthy system now inflates the disagreement counter. Read `stage` too; treat
`dehumidify` as running.

**D6 — MEDIUM. Shutdown budget has zero margin.** `SHUTDOWN_TIMEOUT_S = 30.0` equals
compose's `stop_grace_period: 30s`, so a slow drain races SIGKILL against the WAL
checkpoint the splice procedure assumes. Set 20s vs 45s.

### E. Anomaly detection (goal 2) — does not exist yet; the data is ready

Everything today is manual eyeballing of honest charts. The one real anomaly *signal* —
the HVAC watts-vs-capacity residual on `/ui/hvac` — is computed per page view, never
stored, trended, thresholded, or pushed. Grep for alert/notify/digest across `src/`:
nothing but the meter-staleness field. None of the owner's scenarios would be caught:
water heater element stuck on (duty cycle derivable from `energy_hourly`, underivable by
anything today), strip heat in mild weather (**the best-instrumented case in the system**
— `eheat` kWh/day and `outdoor_temp_f` are both already collected, and nothing joins
them), a failing freezer, phantom-load growth, the barn EV charger outside its 3.6–40
kWh/day envelope. The recent history is the proof: #180's six days of latched zeros were
found by a person reading a chart.

**Simplest thing that closes most of it:** a nightly `digest` job in the existing
scheduler — per-circuit daily kWh and duty cycle from `energy_hourly` vs a trailing
21-day median band, plus ~5 hard rules mapping one-to-one to the scenarios above,
coverage-gated so a gap never fires. **It must push** (SES email or an ntfy topic — one
new dependency), because a field nobody polls is how #180 stayed hidden. Store the
nightly HVAC residual coefficient alongside and the one existing seed becomes a trend.
Second cheap win: `import-greenbutton` a Download-My-Data export back to 2024
(`HistoryLength` is two years) — one command, no code, and "what did the house use last
winter" is answerable at meter grain today instead of in a year.

- **F1 — HIGH.** The nesting-hierarchy trap — the single biggest wrong-number risk

  CTs) — is stated *nowhere an LLM reads*: not in `_RAW_30S_DESCRIPTION`, not in
  `_HOURLY_DESCRIPTION`, not in README's "Reading this data honestly". It lives only in
  channel-map notes (stripped from the parquet) and historyview's docstring. One sentence
  in the hourly/raw comments fixes it.
- **F2 — MEDIUM.** README still denies `energy_meter` exists in three places ("designed,
  not yet collected"; the Athena section lists four tables), and `DATABASE_DESCRIPTION`
  omits the meter dataset and the interval_s trap — an LLM following it concludes bill
  verification is impossible.
- **F3 — MEDIUM.** The #179 correction missed five README spots that still assert "stage
  rows are never written on this system" — including the enum-decode table, the most
  likely lookup target — while the adjacent section now says the opposite. Also stale
  pre-#179 comments in `glue.py:371-376,676-678,941-943`, `channel_map.json:82`, and a
  now-vacuous `test_docs.py` assertion.
- **F4 — MEDIUM.** `dim_channel.category`'s published comment invents example values
  (`kitchen`, `lighting`, `backup-feed`) that don't exist in `KNOWN_CATEGORIES` — a
  `WHERE category='lighting'` returns zero rows and reads as "no lighting circuits".
  Generate the examples from the real set.
- **F5 — MEDIUM.** The MWBC volts-doubling trap (p5/p10/p13 read ~240V on 120V circuits)

- **F6 — LOW.** STATE.md:140 sign error (B0); `compare.py:13` "no S3 yet";

## What was checked and is genuinely solid

Worth recording so future reviews don't re-litigate it:

- **The kWh math and its defenses**: observed-time-only, NULL never 0, no
  spine/COALESCE/LEFT JOIN (grep-tested), dedupe-before-aggregate, single-threaded UTC
  DuckDB for byte-identical re-runs — and it re-derives exactly from raw (verified live).
- **DST handling is excellent**: 23/25-hour days, the fall-back hour kept distinct via
  UTC bucketing, partition math parametrized across 2025-2028, closed-hour logic waits
  for the second 01:00.
- **Atomicity/idempotency**: staged-write-verify-copy-verify at every S3 boundary, the
  delete-on-open `pq.write_table` trap fixed with temp+fsync+replace in both writers,
  deterministic filenames, byte-identical re-run tests at all three stages, crash
  permutations tested.
- **Source fidelity**: None/NaN → gap never zero; 2-pole arithmetic with a missing pole
  gapping the metric rather than halving it; channel identity from `position` (WS
  subscriptions correctly keyed on the mutating API id and re-diffed); the breaker_p0
  guard; enum tables append-only, MappingProxyType, unknown values gap loudly;
  disconnected thermostat → zero rows, not stale rows.
- **Carrier/LG&E auth ladders**: bounded, anti-spin floors, rotation persisted before
  use, secrets scrubbed from exceptions; the #177 breadcrumb works both directions.
- **The meter/table comment discipline**: interval_s double-count, house/barn never-sum,
  retired-id collapse, publication-lag-vs-downtime distinction — accurate and sharp
  where they exist (the gaps are F1/F2, not the meter comments).
- **The uncommitted #179 diff itself is correct and safe** — documentation-only, the
  per-reading classifier was already right in code.
- **Purge interlocks** (first live run 2026-08-24): uploaded AND aged in one SQL
  predicate; cannot delete un-landed data.
- **Loss posture**: since S3 went live, a total Lightsail disk loss costs ≤1 hour of
  raw_30s, one LG&E browser re-auth, and rebuild time.
